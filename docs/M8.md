# M8: the telephone line (Edesy adapter, caller identification, phone takeover)

## 1. Scope, taken from the documents (not invented)
`docs/ROADMAP.md` lists **"M8-M9 | Edesy adapter (approval needed); closure"**, and `docs/M2.md` says *"no caller identity by phone number (M8)"*. The detailed
requirements are in your master specification, sections 5–8:
- **§5.1 / §5.4.** A public Indian number answered by SupportGraph, with Edesy as the recommended provider, behind a **telephony-provider abstraction**. Separate
  layers for PSTN connectivity, call control, media streaming, audio conversion and buffering, AI orchestration, human intervention, staff audio and call lifecycle.
  The LangGraph workflow stays provider-independent.
- **§5.3.** The demo must not depend on a paid service or an expiring trial. A public number is optional and can be enabled later.
- **§6.** Identify the caller from the number (student, staff, administrator, unknown). The rules:
  - caller ID is **not** proof of identity;
  - no sensitive data on a number match alone;
  - extra verification is required;
  - RBAC is enforced outside the LLM.
- **§7 / §8.** Live monitoring, listening and takeover of phone calls without a second call leg, with the original PSTN connection kept. The Trial Verification
  disclosure and consent also apply to phone calls.

**What M8 does not include, and why.**
- **"Closure" (M9)** is a separate roadmap item.
- **Answering on an ordinary mobile phone (§7)** is not part of M8. Edesy documents no API for bridging a staff member's own phone into an existing media stream
  without a second leg. Staff take over in the browser, as for web calls.
- **No provider other than Edesy.** Your rule: *"Do not silently replace Edesy or implement another provider"*.

**Discrepancy.** The roadmap groups "M8-M9" in one row. I treated M8 as the Edesy adapter plus caller identification and phone takeover, and left the closure work
to M9.

## 3. How a phone call runs
```
caller ──PSTN──> Edesy ──wss (L16 8/16 kHz)──> /api/telephony/edesy/stream/<secret>
                                                   │  PhoneBridge (resample, 20 ms frames, killAudio / disconnect / mark)
                                                   ▼
                                    call hub (M4/M4.1/M5): Live Calls, listen, takeover, hand-over announcement, trial disclosure, consent
                                                   │
                                    VoiceSession (VAD, STT, one turn at a time, TTS)  ──>  LangGraph support graph (unchanged)
```
- **Same machinery as the browser voice line.** A phone call is the same kind of `calls` row (`channel = 'phone'`) and uses the same hub, session and graph.
  - The hub's no-overlap invariant is carried onto the line. On barge-in, and on every ownership change, the bridge sends `killAudio`, so Edesy drops any AI audio
    it still has queued before the hand-over announcement and the staff voice.
  - When staff end the call, Edesy gets `disconnect`.
- **No second call leg.** Staff audio (16 kHz from the browser) is resampled and sent down the **same** WebSocket. The caller stays on the original PSTN leg the
  whole time. That leg is still billed per minute by Edesy; M8 avoids an *extra* transfer leg, not the call charge itself.
- **Keypad instead of buttons.**
  - The opening prompt explains `*` + PIN + `#`.
  - In Trial Verification Mode with recording offered, the caller hears the disclosure, then *"To allow recording, press 1. To continue without recording,
    press 2. You can press 3 at any time to stop recording."*
  - The result is spoken back ("Recording is on." / "Recording is off.").
  - Consent still goes through `TrialCall.handle` with the current policy version, so the M5 rules apply unchanged: explicit, never inferred, withdrawable.
- **Playback acknowledgement.** After each response the bridge sends a `mark`. When Edesy reports it played out, the session gets the same "played" acknowledgement a
  browser sends, so its latency metrics stay meaningful.
- **Keys are handled in order, one at a time** (a per-call queue, bounded to 64 keys).

## 4. Caller identification and verification (§6)
**1. Normalise** `from` to E.164 (Indian default: `9845012345`, `09845012345`, `919845012345`, `+91 98450 12345` all become `+919845012345`).

**2. Look up who the number *claims* to be:**
- students through `students.phone`;
- staff and administrators through `account_phones`, which an administrator sets;
- disabled accounts are ignored.

The result is `unknown`, `claimed` (exactly one account) or `ambiguous` (several accounts). It is stored on the call together with the claimed role, a **masked**
number (`+91 ******2345`) and a per-tenant keyed hash. The raw number is never stored or logged.

**3. Every phone call starts anonymous.** A claimed caller gets exactly what a signed-out web visitor gets: public answers only. The graph's tools are scoped by the
`student_id` the code passes. The language model never sees the claim and cannot raise its own permissions.

**4. Proof is the account's phone PIN** (`*` digits `#`):
- **Correct PIN:**
  - **student:** the call's identity switches to that student, with a new ticket bound to them. The call row becomes `verified` and the event
    `caller_verified {role}` is written. Only after this can the student hear their own records.
  - **staff or administrator:** marked verified, but they get **no extra powers by phone**. Staff tools stay in the web console.
- **Wrong PIN, no PIN set, or unknown number:** all sound the same ("That PIN is not correct") and take the same time.
- **Ambiguous (shared) number:** cannot be verified by PIN at all. The caller is offered a person.

**5. Lockout.**
- 5 wrong PINs in a row (`SG_PHONE_PIN_MAX_ATTEMPTS`) lock phone verification for 15 minutes (`SG_PHONE_PIN_LOCK_MINUTES`). Each call allows at most 3 attempts.
- The attempt is **reserved before** the hash is compared: one `UPDATE … SET failed_attempts = failed_attempts + 1 WHERE <not locked> RETURNING`. On
  PostgreSQL that row stays locked to the end of the transaction, so parallel calls are checked one after another. A guess arriving after the limit is refused
  without being checked.
- The lockout never affects the web sign-in. An administrator can reset the PIN.
- **A bug found and fixed while testing.** The first version counted the failure *after* the comparison. The PostgreSQL race test showed 8 parallel guesses all
  being checked, 3 more than the limit. Reserving the attempt first fixed it.

**6. Setting a PIN.**
- The account holder sets it under the chat (or in the admin tab), after re-entering their **password**. Wrong passwords are rate-limited.
- 6 digits (`SG_PHONE_PIN_LENGTH`). Runs like 123456 and repeated digits like 111111 are refused.
- It is stored only as a salted scrypt hash, and is never shown again or written to the audit.

**Known weakness, stated.** Someone who spoofs a student's number can lock that student's *phone* verification for 15 minutes by guessing badly. It costs the
student nothing else, and an administrator can reset it. A PIN typed on a keypad can also be overheard. Both are acceptable for a demo; for production, see §9.

## 5. Security
| Concern | What is done |
|---|---|
| Edesy does not authenticate the media WebSocket (its docs say nothing) | The URL carries a secret of at least 32 characters (`SG_TELEPHONY_STREAM_TOKEN`), compared in constant time; any mismatch is refused before the handshake. There is an optional source-IP allowlist (`SG_TELEPHONY_ALLOWED_IPS`). The status API never shows the token. Use `wss://` only (`edesy_setup.py` refuses `ws://`). |
| Flooding | `SG_TELEPHONY_MAX_CONCURRENT` (default 4) simultaneous phone calls; the chat rate limit applies per caller; DTMF is a bounded queue; 10 s to send the first frame; malformed metadata or an unsupported rate is refused. |
| Spoofed caller ID | Section 4: a claim only, never proof. |
| Prompt injection by voice | Unchanged: the same guardrails as text and voice. Identity is decided outside the model. |
| RBAC / tenant / IDOR | The admin APIs are `require_admin`, scoped to the token's tenant (another tenant's account → 404). A student's number cannot be set here (422): it is their own record, changed only through their confirmed request. Each account sets only its own PIN. |
| Audit | Call events `caller_identified {caller_status}`, `caller_verification_failed {result}` and `caller_verified {role}`. `telephony_audit` records phone set/removed, PIN set/cleared/reset and lockouts. Neither ever holds a number or a PIN. |
| Data minimisation | Calls keep a masked number and a keyed hash; staff see the masked number only. |

## 6. Configuration (`.env`, all optional; defaults keep the line off)
```
SG_TELEPHONY_PROVIDER=none          # edesy to enable (paid: see section 7)
SG_TELEPHONY_STREAM_TOKEN=          # >= 32 chars: python -c "import secrets; print(secrets.token_urlsafe(32))"
SG_TELEPHONY_SAMPLE_RATE=16000      # 8000 or 16000; must match `make edesy-attach RATE=...`
SG_TELEPHONY_TENANT=qspiders_sample
SG_TELEPHONY_NUMBER_TENANTS=        # 917969002802=qspiders_sample,...
SG_TELEPHONY_ALLOWED_IPS=           # optional
SG_TELEPHONY_MAX_CONCURRENT=4
SG_PHONE_PIN_LENGTH=6  SG_PHONE_PIN_MAX_ATTEMPTS=5  SG_PHONE_PIN_LOCK_MINUTES=15  SG_PHONE_PIN_ATTEMPTS_PER_CALL=3
```

## 7. Cost and approval
- **Free path (no approval needed).**
  - `make phone-sim` speaks the Edesy protocol to your local API: metadata, L16 audio at 8/16 kHz, DTMF, playback markers.
  - It exercises the entire phone path, including Live Calls and takeover, without a number or a provider.
- **Paid path (needs your approval).** A public Indian number through Edesy means:
  - number rental;
  - per-minute charges (the figures you found, ₹0.50 and ₹0.80 per minute, conflict; I did not re-verify them in this round);
  - GST;
  - KYC.

  The code is ready, but **nothing in it enables or pays for Edesy**. You decide.
- **The steps, once approved:**
  1. Expose the API over TLS: a tunnel or a reverse proxy with a certificate.
  2. Set the `.env` lines above.
  3. Run `export EDESY_API_KEY=…`, then `make edesy-numbers`, `make edesy-stream-test URL=wss://…/api/telephony/edesy/stream/<token>` and
     `make edesy-attach NUMBER_ID=… URL=…`.
  4. Undo with `make edesy-detach`.

## 8. Trying it for free
```
# .env (local only): SG_TELEPHONY_PROVIDER=edesy  SG_TELEPHONY_STREAM_TOKEN=<32+ random chars>
make api
make phone-sim ARGS='--from "+91 98450 12345" --rate 8000 --wav question.wav --keys "*482913#" --out reply.wav'
```
- Give a student account a number (the student record's phone), and set its PIN under **Chat → Phone support PIN**.
- Watch the call in **Staff console → Live Calls**: it shows "Phone · not verified" until the PIN is typed.
- Take it over: the simulator prints `killAudio`, and your voice arrives in `reply.wav`.

## 9. Limitations and production notes (stated, not worked around)
- **Not tested against Edesy.** The adapter follows Edesy's published protocol (checked 2026-11-12). It was tested only against a fake stream in the sandbox; no
  call reached Edesy and no number was used.
- **Undocumented behaviour.** Edesy does not document frame sizes beyond "~20 ms", its buffering depth, or how fast `killAudio` takes effect. Check the first real
  call: a long audible tail after a barge-in would point to Edesy's buffer.
- **Transcript after verification.** It continues in the verified student's conversation. Staff see the part after verification in the call transcript; the
  anonymous part before it stays in the anonymous conversation record.
- **Legal approval.** Telephone recording and consent (Indian telecom and DPDP rules) and keypad PIN disclosure need QSpiders' approval before a public deployment.
  The M5 notice text is yours to approve; nothing here is legal advice.
- **Production upgrades:**
  - stronger verification (an OTP to the registered number via SMS, which is a paid service and needs your approval);
  - a provider-signed WebSocket, if Edesy adds one;
  - number-level fraud monitoring.
